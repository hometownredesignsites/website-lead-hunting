# Visual opportunity scoring

This rubric is used by the ChatGPT visual-review workflow. Scripted technical priority remains a separate initial measure. The visual review receives structured script-audit evidence and four screenshot views: desktop and mobile top, plus a content-aware mid-page view for each viewport. When page-flow evidence is available, the mid crop targets the first substantial project, portfolio, gallery, or service image; otherwise it selects the first substantial below-the-fold image, then falls back to a scroll-position crop. Fresh captures use live page-flow measurements to select and scroll to the target, and record the selected image and position in `recapture-manifest.json`. Before assessment, a capture-validation step requires the mid crop to be at least 150 pixels below the top crop, verifies it is not an identical image, and checks measured browser scroll movement on fresh captures. If a distinct mid view cannot be verified, the candidate is marked failed with diagnostics and ChatGPT is not sent duplicate screenshots. Page-flow measurements separately record where the first meaningful image and contact form occur.

## Required factors

Each completed assessment must classify all seven factors with an explicit assessment state and specific evidence or a reason the factor cannot be rated:

| Factor | Evidence source | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero imagery | Screenshots plus scripted video-presence override | Presence and presentation of meaningful first-view imagery |
| Image quality | Screenshots plus scripted hero-video override | Visible blur, pixelation, stretching, poor crops, and quality across the portfolio |
| Design freshness | Screenshots | Concrete signs that the overall visual language looks dated; not trend conformity |
| Readability | Screenshots | Legibility of visible text, including text over imagery |
| Layout and mobile use | Screenshots | Hierarchy, content organization, and desktop/mobile usability |
| Broken images | Script audit; screenshots when the audit is inconclusive | Source-backed failed loads, lazy-image sample coverage, or visible failures in the four captured views |
| Interactions | Script audit | Tested controls and interaction failures |

The model must not describe script findings as visually observed. Visual observations and script evidence remain separately identified in the scorecard. When the script identifies a substantial video in the first viewport, Python deterministically sets both hero imagery and image quality to assessed/none (zero points). Do not leave image quality uncertain because still screenshots do not show project photos; the rubric assumes the video can present multiple examples. This override does not neutralize other factors.

For broken images, every discovered lazy-loaded image is inspected when there are 20 or fewer. The bounded scroll pass also tries common deferred-source attributes such as `data-src` when native `loading=lazy` is not set. If the page has more than 20 lazy images, a 20-image sample is sufficient evidence for that sample; remaining lazy images do not make the scorecard uncertain. An image is counted as failed only when it has a real requested source, is visible, and its load failed, or when a browser request failure matches a DOM image URL. An empty `src`/`currentSrc`, hidden element, deferred URL that never became a request, or uncorrelated ORB/cross-origin error is recorded as unresolved diagnostic evidence, not a confirmed customer-facing failure. `ERR_ABORTED` cancellations are retained as transient diagnostics. If the image audit is partial, inconclusive, or unavailable and all four screenshots were confirmed uploaded, ChatGPT may assess broken-image evidence from the visible images in those screenshots. A screenshot fallback is limited to the captured views; it does not certify images elsewhere on the site. Use `Uncertain` only when neither the source-backed audit nor the supplied screenshots can support a defensible factor assessment. The scorecard records the evidence source and whether screenshot fallback was used.

Each factor stores `state` as `assessed`, `not_applicable`, or `uncertain`. Assessed factors also store severity (`none`, `minor`, `moderate`, or `severe`); not-applicable and uncertain factors have no severity. Every factor stores a concrete evidence statement and its required source. The parser accepts common source-label spelling variants, uses labeled factor text over a duplicate structured JSON copy, and records the canonical source according to the factor schema; formatting differences alone do not invalidate otherwise complete evidence. “No control was tested” is not evidence that controls work.

The preliminary script audit also checks for image-enlargement affordances on the homepage. When it sees at least four substantial images and finds no direct-image link or explicit lightbox/zoom markup, it adds a small technical-opportunity signal. This is a bounded heuristic: JavaScript can provide a lightbox without an obvious markup hint, so “not detected” is not proof that enlargement is unavailable. It is not one of the seven visual factors and does not establish that an image interaction is broken.

For **design freshness**, require concrete evidence from typography, effects, controls, spacing, iconography, or the overall visual language. Minor means one limited outdated detail. Moderate requires at least two concrete dated-pattern cues recurring across at least two prominent areas; severe means several cues are pervasive and materially weaken credibility. Name the cues, screenshot/page areas, and user impact. A palette, serif type, black panels, basic buttons, repeated styling by itself, or personal preference is not enough. Treat copyright years as a separate weak footer clue; neither a current generated year nor an old displayed year determines the design-freshness rating.

The reviewer-approved dated-design anchor is a site whose oversized serif/gold branding, heavy dark panels, and basic rectangular controls recur across the hero, gallery, and contact areas. That combined pattern is moderate and receives the 45-point design floor. Do not generalize from any single color or styling choice. The reviewed polished contemporary-site examples anchor `none`: clear hierarchy, strong imagery, coherent typography and spacing, and refined controls. The model should assess the visible evidence against these traits rather than trying to copy a particular site's template.

For **image quality**, score what is visibly apparent in the screenshots unless the first-view video rule applies. An isolated weak image is minor; several prominent images that are blurry, pixelated, stretched, or poorly cropped are moderate; low-quality imagery that dominates the hero or portfolio is severe. This can be a redesign opportunity even when every image loads successfully. Do not infer quality from source filenames or pixel dimensions alone. Avoid double-counting: use hero imagery for first-view composition, image quality for the images themselves, and design freshness for the site's broader visual language.

## Durable score

The validator calculates the score from these fixed factor ratings, not from how many issue sentences or clusters the model writes:

| Factor severity | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| None | 0 |
| Minor | 5 |
| Moderate | 25 |
| Severe | 50 |

If the script confirms the first viewport has no prominent image, video, or background image, the hero-imagery factor receives at least 45 points. A substantial video element in the first viewport is treated as present and assumed to play, even if the capture browser has not decoded a frame; Python forces both hero-imagery and image-quality to `assessed: none` (0 points), clearing image-quality uncertainty caused by missing still project photos. The page-flow scan also retains visible video elements as capture targets when their frames have not decoded. A moderate or severe design-freshness finding also receives at least 45 points: that signals a repeated or pervasive dated visual language and represents a redesign opportunity on par with missing hero media. Each floor replaces a lower value within its own factor; it does not count the same issue twice. A minor, isolated outdated detail remains 5 points. Other independently evidenced factor issues add their points.

`score = min(100, sum(points for assessed factors after the applicable hero-media and dated-design floors) + stale-copyright points)`

Verdict bands remain Pass 0–49, Medium 50–74, and High 75–100. ChatGPT returns factor evidence only. Python is the sole authority for the numeric score and site outcome. `review_execution_status` records whether ChatGPT returned a response, `scorecard_validation_status` records whether that response passed validation, retry status records whether another attempt is needed, and `site_outcome` records High, Medium, Pass, or Uncertain. These fields describe separate things; a completed review can still have an invalid or uncertain scorecard.

An uncertain state on any required factor yields an Uncertain site outcome with a null score. A not-applicable factor contributes zero only when there is evidence that the check truly does not apply. For interactions, no discovered safe controls means not applicable; discovered controls left untested means uncertain; tested controls are assessed from their observed results. Missing factor states, severities for assessed factors, evidence, or source invalidate the scorecard and trigger the existing failure/retry path. Explicit model score/verdict headings and prose that contradicts a factor's assessment also invalidate the scorecard. Invalid responses are not saved as numeric site ratings. The validator never silently assigns zero to missing evidence.

## Durable evidence

For each candidate, capture validation runs before image assessment. A passing validation establishes one four-image set: desktop top/mid and mobile top/mid. Three independent ChatGPT replicates receive the exact same four files; each session has a fresh Xvfb display and browser profile. A recoverable invalid or interrupted replicate is retried in a new session up to two times, without rerunning replicates that already passed. This isolates variation in model/session responses from variation in screenshots and bounds retries to at most three sessions per replicate.

All three replicates must produce valid, non-uncertain scorecards before a durable aggregate rating is written. Python calculates the arithmetic mean and score range. When the range is **30 points or more**, the highest replicate becomes the applied score only if its scorecard is valid and its scored factor has specific supporting evidence. For a dated-design override, the evidence must name at least two concrete dated cues and show them in at least two page areas/screenshots. If this evidence gate fails, Python retains the rounded mean and flags the unresolved disagreement in the master; it does not silently apply the maximum. The private master preserves each replicate's score, factor points, evidence support, the mean, min/max/range, applied method, selected replicate when applicable, and both applied and mean factor breakdowns. The high/medium CSV is a concise shortlist and omits per-replicate status/factor JSON and detailed aggregation diagnostics. Verdict bands remain Pass 0–49, Medium 50–74, and High 75–100. If capture validation fails, images are not submitted. If a replicate remains invalid or uncertain after its bounded fresh-session retries, the candidate receives no aggregate score and enters the existing delayed candidate-retry or failed path. That candidate-level retry currently repeats the full three-replicate set; successful replicates are reused across retries within the same job only.

Each scorecard record stores process status separately from validation status, retry state, and site outcome, alongside normalized factor states and severities, factor evidence and source, structured script-audit summary, page-flow measurements, screenshot observations, explanatory issue notes, the Python-calculated score/verdict when valid, and the complete raw model responses. Invalid or incomplete replicate sets are not accepted as numeric results. The private candidate ledger retains these durable results. Screenshots and detailed browser diagnostics remain in the public workflow's seven-day artifact. No post-evaluation provider-specific weight or score offset is applied.

The private `git-chatgpt` branch also maintains `scores/SCORING_MASTER.csv` and `scores/HIGH_MEDIUM_CANDIDATES.csv`. The master is updated immediately after each replicate and includes one row per normalized website host, individual replicate scores and factor-point breakdowns, running mean, and confidence level equal to the count of valid replicates (1–3). One- and two-replicate means are provisional; three valid replicates mark the score complete. The second CSV contains only current High and Medium outcomes, including provisional High/Medium results. Passes, uncertain results, and incomplete reviews remain visible in the master. Full evidence remains in the private run ledger and per-run scorecard records.

In the private scoring CSVs, `provisional_outcome` is the current rating while valid replicates accumulate, `final_outcome` is populated only after all three valid replicates, and `outcome` is the current rating used for High/Medium filtering. The writer currently sets `outcome` equal to `provisional_outcome`, so those two columns duplicate one another; `final_outcome` distinguishes completed assessments from provisional ones.

## Human-label calibration

The benchmark joins the interactive `decision` field to the newest provider scorecards by normalized host. It reports coverage, per-label outcome comparisons, provider agreement, and score differences. Use a deterministic, stratified holdout for evaluation; do not use holdout sites to tune factors or thresholds. The current human file has 21 High and 42 Medium labels, no Pass examples. The v13 rubric gives prominent first-view video zero hero-imagery and image-quality points, applies a 45-point floor to confirmed absent first-view hero media and moderate/severe dated design, scores image quality and design freshness explicitly, requires factor state/evidence validation, treats cancelled image requests as transient diagnostics, distinguishes source-backed image failures from empty or uncorrelated failures, permits screenshot-only fallback when all four captures are confirmed uploaded, and accepts a complete clean lazy-image audit even when another bounded check makes the overall audit partial. It also requires multiple concrete, cross-section cues for moderate/severe datedness and uses a guarded highest-replicate rule for score ranges of 30+ points. Historical v3 through v12 scores are excluded from current-version benchmark comparisons; rerun candidates under v13 before using them for calibration. The reviewer-approved dated-design anchor is the repeated combination of oversized serif/gold branding, heavy dark panels, and basic rectangular controls across multiple major page areas. Do not claim balanced accuracy or threshold quality until the remaining classes are evaluated, and add human-reviewed Pass examples.
